How to Start a Window Cleaning Business

Updated November 13, 2026 · 3 min read

Window cleaning is one of the lowest-cost service businesses to start. A few professional tools, a ladder and a way to get around are enough for your first jobs. The money is in recurring customers on a tight route. Here's how to set it up.

1. Get the right tools

  • Squeegees in a few sizes, with quality rubber
  • Strip washers (scrubbers) and a bucket
  • Window cleaning solution (many pros use a little dish soap in water)
  • Scrapers for paint and debris, used carefully to avoid scratching glass
  • Microfiber towels and detailing cloths
  • A tool belt and holster
  • Extension poles
  • Ladders: a step ladder and an extension ladder with stabilizers
  • Later: a water-fed pole system with purified water for high windows

2. Make safety a habit

Falls are the biggest risk in this trade. Set ladders at the right angle on firm ground, use stabilizers, don't overreach, and use water-fed poles instead of ladders for upper floors where you can. Decide what heights you will and won't do.

3. Handle the business basics

Check local business license requirements, register your business name, get an EIN and open a business bank account. Buy general liability insurance; broken windows and damaged screens happen.

4. Set prices

Price per window or per pane, add screens, tracks, height and hard water stains, and set a minimum charge. See how much to charge for window cleaning for example rates.

5. Quote accurately

Count windows by type and floor using a window count sheet, or ask customers for photos of each side of the house. Put the quote in writing with what's included (inside, outside, screens, tracks) and what isn't.

6. Use an agreement and a checklist

A simple agreement covers weather rescheduling, access, pets, breakage policy and payment terms. A job checklist keeps your work consistent: screens off and cleaned, glass, frames and sills wiped, tracks, screens back on, final walkaround with the customer.

7. Find customers

  • Before-and-after photos on your Google Business Profile and Nextdoor
  • Door hangers on streets where you already have a job
  • Storefronts for weekly or monthly glass cleaning, which builds a steady route
  • Realtors for listing prep
  • House cleaners and pressure washers who can refer you, and vice versa
  • Seasonal promotions in spring and before the holidays

8. Build a recurring route

Convert one-time customers to quarterly or twice-yearly plans. Group customers by neighborhood so each day has minimal driving. A dense route is the difference between a side hustle and a full-time income.

Launch checklist

  • Professional tools and ladders
  • Safety rules for heights
  • License, EIN, bank account and insurance
  • Price list and minimum charge
  • Window count sheet, agreement and job checklist
  • Google Business Profile with photos
  • Recurring plan offer
