How to Price a Painting Job (Interior and Exterior)

Updated November 13, 2026 · 3 min read

Painting estimates go wrong in two ways: underestimating prep, and forgetting the small stuff like doors, trim and ceilings. A simple, repeatable method fixes both. Here's how to price residential painting so your bids are competitive and profitable.

The basic formula

Price = labor + materials + overhead + profit

  • Labor: hours to prep, paint and clean up, times your labor rate
  • Materials: paint, primer, caulk, tape, plastic, patching compound, sundries
  • Overhead: insurance, vehicle, equipment, advertising, office time
  • Profit: the business's margin on top, separate from your pay

Many painters find that materials are only a fraction of the job price; labor is the big number. That's why accurate hour estimates matter more than anything.

Step 1: Measure

For interior walls, measure the perimeter of each room and multiply by the wall height to get wall square footage. Subtract large openings if you like, but many painters leave small windows and doors in as a buffer for cutting in. Measure ceilings separately (length × width), and count doors, windows, and linear feet of baseboard and trim.

For exteriors, measure each side of the house (width × height to the eaves), add gables, and count windows, doors, shutters and trim.

Step 2: Estimate hours with production rates

A production rate is how much you paint per hour. Track your own; until you have data, use conservative assumptions. For example, one painter rolling walls might cover roughly 150 to 250 sq ft per hour per coat after prep, much less when cutting in around lots of trim.

Add separate time for:

  • Prep: moving furniture, covering floors, patching holes, sanding, caulking, priming stains
  • Ceilings: slower than walls
  • Doors: often priced per door, per side
  • Trim and baseboards: priced per linear foot or by time
  • Color changes: dark to light usually needs an extra coat or primer
  • Setup and cleanup each day

Step 3: Calculate materials

Divide the area by the paint's coverage per gallon (on the can, often around 350 to 400 sq ft per gallon) and multiply by the number of coats. Add primer where needed and a sundries allowance. Many painters mark up materials 10% to 20% to cover pickup time and waste.

Example: one bedroom repaint

Item Assumption Notes
Walls 12 × 12 room, 8 ft ceilings = 384 sq ft, 2 coats Labor and about 2 gallons
Ceiling 144 sq ft, 1 coat Separate labor line
Doors 2 doors, both sides Per-door price
Trim 48 linear ft baseboard Per-foot price
Prep Patch nail holes, caulk trim, cover floor Often 1 to 2 hours

Total each line, add overhead and profit, and you have a price you can defend.

Present the estimate clearly

List the rooms or surfaces, what's included (prep, coats, colors, brands), what's excluded (moving heavy furniture, drywall repair beyond patching, wallpaper removal), the total, deposit and payment schedule, and how long the quote is valid. Attach a color schedule so the right color goes on the right wall.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the walkthrough and pricing from photos
  • Underpricing prep, which often takes as long as painting
  • Free "while you're here" extras that add hours
  • No written change order when the client adds a room
