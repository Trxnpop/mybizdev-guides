How to Start a Dog Grooming Business (Home, Salon or Mobile)

Updated November 13, 2026 · 3 min read

Dog grooming has something many service businesses don't: built-in repeat business. Most dogs need grooming every four to eight weeks for their whole lives. Build a book of loyal clients and your calendar fills itself. Here's how to get started.

1. Pick your setup

Setup Startup cost Pros Cons
Home-based Lowest No rent, flexible hours Zoning rules, limited space
Mobile van or trailer Highest Premium prices, no rent Vehicle cost, water and power logistics
Salon suite or rented station Medium Professional space, walk-in visibility Monthly rent

Check zoning rules before grooming from home; some areas restrict home businesses with customer visits.

2. Get trained

Grooming is skilled, physical work. Most new groomers learn through a grooming school, an apprenticeship or both. Training covers breed cuts, safe handling, recognizing skin problems and using clippers and dryers safely. Certification isn't required everywhere, but it builds client trust.

3. Handle licensing and insurance

  • Check city and county business license requirements, and any state or local rules for animal businesses
  • Register your business name and get an EIN
  • Open a business bank account
  • Get insurance that covers injury to pets in your care, plus general liability

4. Buy core equipment

Grooming table with a safety arm, professional clippers and blades, shears (straight, curved, thinning), brushes and combs, a high-velocity dryer, tub or bathing station, nail clippers and grinder, shampoos and conditioners, ear cleaner, towels, and kennels or crates. Keep blades sharp and equipment clean.

5. Set up your paperwork

Every dog should have a file. You need:

  • New client and pet form: owner details, emergency contact, vet, vaccinations, health issues and behavior
  • Grooming release: covers matted coats, senior or anxious dogs and medical emergencies
  • Grooming record: the cut, blade lengths and notes, so every groom is consistent
  • Pup report card: a fun note for the owner that shows what you noticed (skin, ears, nails)
  • Pickup and drop-off log if you kennel dogs
  • Invoice

6. Set prices and policies

Price by size, coat and condition, add matting and handling fees, and set no-show and late pickup policies. See how much to charge for dog grooming for example rates.

7. Find your first regulars

  • Google Business Profile with before-and-after photos
  • Instagram and Facebook: groomed dogs are made for social media
  • Vets, pet stores, trainers and doggy daycares: leave cards and offer referral perks
  • Pre-book every client for their next appointment before they leave
  • Puppy intro grooms to build lifelong clients

8. Track appointments and money

Log every groom, payment and expense. Watch which services and breeds earn the most per hour, and adjust prices accordingly.

Launch checklist

  • Choose home, salon or mobile and check zoning
  • Complete training
  • License, EIN, bank account and insurance
  • Core equipment
  • Client form, grooming release, grooming record and report cards
  • Price grid and policies
  • Google Business Profile and social media
