How to Start a Pet Sitting or Dog Walking Business

Updated November 12, 2026 · 3 min read

If you already sit for friends or pick up jobs through a booking app, going independent is the natural next step. You keep the whole fee, set your own policies and build a client list that is actually yours. Here's how to set it up properly.

1. Decide on your services

  • Drop-in visits for cats and dogs while owners work or travel
  • Dog walking, solo or small groups
  • Overnight stays in the client's home
  • House sitting with pet care, plants and mail
  • Specialty care such as senior pets, medication, puppies or exotic pets

Many sitters start with drop-ins and walks during the week and overnights on weekends and holidays.

2. Set up the business properly

  • Check whether your city or county needs a business license. Some areas also limit the number of dogs one walker can handle.
  • Register your business name and get a free EIN.
  • Open a separate bank account.
  • Get pet sitter insurance, which typically covers injury to pets in your care and damage to client homes. Bonding is common if you hire help.
  • Consider a pet first aid and CPR course. Clients notice it, and it may save a life.

3. Set your rates and policies

Price by visit length and type, then add fees for extra pets, holidays and last-minute bookings. See how much to charge for pet sitting for example rates. Decide your cancellation policy, deposit rules for holidays, and when payment is due before you take your first client.

4. Prepare your paperwork

Professional forms protect you and reassure owners:

  • New client form with home access and emergency contacts
  • Pet profile with feeding, meds and behavior
  • Vet and emergency release
  • Service agreement
  • Visit report card
  • Invoice

5. Always do a meet-and-greet

Before the first booking, visit the client at home. Meet the pet, learn the routine, test the keys or codes, review the forms and get signatures. If the pet seems aggressive or the situation feels wrong, it's okay to decline.

6. Build safety habits

  • Use a secure leash and check harness fit every walk.
  • Never let dogs off leash unless the owner has agreed in writing and it's legal and safe.
  • Keep emergency vet addresses saved on your phone.
  • Double-check doors, gates and windows before leaving.
  • Send a quick update with a photo after every visit. It builds trust fast.

7. Move clients off the apps (the right way)

Booking apps are great for building reviews, but they take a cut and often have rules against moving clients off the platform. Read the terms of any app you use. A common approach is to build your independent brand separately: your own Google Business Profile, a simple website, a business card at every visit, and referrals from happy clients.

8. Find local clients

  • Google Business Profile with photos of happy pets (with owner permission)
  • Nextdoor and neighborhood Facebook groups
  • Vets, groomers, pet stores and dog parks: ask to leave cards
  • Apartment buildings that allow pets
  • Referral rewards, like a free walk for every new client referred

9. Track bookings and money

Keep a schedule you can trust and log every visit and payment. Holidays get chaotic; a clear weekly schedule prevents double-booking. Set aside money for taxes from every payment.

Launch checklist

  • Choose services and service area
  • Check local license and dog-limit rules
  • Buy pet sitter insurance
  • Set rates, cancellation and payment policies
  • Prepare client, pet profile, vet release, agreement and report card forms
  • Create your Google Business Profile
  • Leave cards with vets, groomers and pet stores
