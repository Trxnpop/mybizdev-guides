How Much to Charge for Snow Removal (Per Push, Per Visit and Seasonal)

Updated November 19, 2026 · 3 min read

Snow removal pricing is tricky because you are selling something nobody can predict: the weather. Charge per visit and a mild winter leaves you broke. Charge a flat season and a record snowfall leaves you working for free. The answer is to pick the right pricing model for each customer and put the rules in writing.

The three ways to charge

Per push (or per visit). You clear the property every time snow passes a trigger depth, usually 2 inches, and bill each visit. Fair for everyone, but your income swings with the weather.

Per inch tiers. Same as per push, but the price steps up for heavier storms, for example one price for 2 to 6 inches and a higher price for 6 to 12 inches. This protects you on big storms when a driveway takes twice as long.

Seasonal contract. The customer pays one price for the whole season, usually in two to four installments, and you clear every qualifying snowfall. Steady income for you, predictable bills for them. Most contracts include a cap (for example, up to 25 pushes or 60 inches total) with a per-push rate after that.

Many operators offer all three and let customers choose.

Example residential starting rates

Prices vary a lot by region, so check what local plow services charge. These are common starting points:

Service Typical starting price
Single-car driveway, per push $30 to $50
Two-car driveway, per push $40 to $75
Long or large driveway $75 to $150+
Sidewalk and front walk (shovel or blower) $15 to $40 add-on
Steps and porch $10 to $25 add-on
Salt or ice melt application $15 to $50 per visit
Heavy storm surcharge (over 6 inches) add 25% to 50%
Seasonal contract, average driveway $300 to $800 per season

How to set your seasonal price

Look up the average number of snow events in your town that reach your trigger depth. Your local weather service or a quick search for your city's average annual snowfall will get you close.

Then multiply: average plowable events × per-push price × 0.85 to 0.9. The small discount is the customer's reason to commit early. Add salting if they want it on every visit.

Example: your area averages 12 events of 2 inches or more. A two-car driveway is $50 per push. 12 × $50 = $600, and with a 10% seasonal discount the contract is $540, billed in three payments of $180.

Price by time, not just size

Driveway size is a starting point, but what really sets the price is how long the job takes. Things that add time:

  • Cars that have to be worked around
  • Steep slopes that need salting every visit
  • No place to pile snow
  • Long walkways, back patios and mailbox clearing
  • Gravel driveways (you need to raise the plow or blower)

Walk each property before the first snow and note these on a site map. In a storm you will not remember which house has the hidden drain grate.

What a snow contract needs

A one-page seasonal agreement prevents most disputes. Include:

  • Trigger depth and how soon after the snow stops you will arrive
  • What is included (driveway, walks, steps, salting) and what is extra
  • Season dates, payment schedule and the per-push rate after any cap
  • Who is responsible for marking obstacles like curbs, sprinkler heads and landscape lights
  • That you are not liable for ice that forms after your visit

Build a route before the first storm

Group customers by neighborhood and set a route order. Snow removal is profitable when you clear five driveways on one street, not five spread across town. Seasonal customers go first, per-push customers next.
