How Much to Charge for Spray Tans (Studio, Mobile and Bridal)

Updated November 19, 2026 · 3 min read

Spray tanning has one of the lowest startup costs in beauty: a machine, a pop-up tent, solution and training. That makes it a great business to start, and also means plenty of people undercharge. Before you set your price list, work out what each tan actually costs you and what clients in your area are used to paying.

Know your cost per tan

Your direct costs per client are small but they add up:

  • Solution: a full-body tan typically uses a few ounces of solution. Divide what you paid for a bottle by the number of tans you get from it.
  • Disposables: barrier cream, sticky feet, hair caps, towels or wipes.
  • Travel: for mobile tans, gas and the time spent driving.
  • Booking and payment fees.

Most artists find their product cost is a small slice of the price. The bigger cost is your time, including setup, cleanup and travel. Price for the whole hour, not just the ten minutes of spraying.

Example starting rates

Rates vary a lot between cities and small towns, so check what local studios and mobile artists charge. Common starting points in the US:

Service Typical starting price
Single full-body tan, in studio $35 to $60
Single tan, mobile (at client's home) $50 to $85
Rapid or express solution add $5 to $15
Contouring or custom color add $10 to $25
Travel fee beyond your free radius $10 to $30
Bridal party, per person (on location, minimum 3 to 5 people) $40 to $70
Bride trial tan $40 to $60
Package of 5 tans 10% to 15% off the single price
Monthly membership (2 tans a month) $70 to $110

Mobile and event pricing

Mobile tans are worth more because you're bringing the studio to the client. Set a free radius (for example 10 miles) and add a travel fee beyond it. For groups, set a minimum booking so a single-person trip across town doesn't eat your afternoon.

Bridal parties and events (proms, competitions, pageants, holiday parties) are the most profitable bookings you'll get. Ask for a deposit to hold the date, set a per-person price with a group minimum, and offer a trial tan for the bride a few weeks before the wedding.

Packages and memberships keep your calendar full

One-time clients come and go. Regulars who tan every two weeks are the backbone of a spray tan business. Two ways to keep them coming back:

  • Packages: prepaid bundles of 3, 5 or 10 tans at a discount. You get paid up front and they have a reason to book again.
  • Memberships: a monthly fee for a set number of tans. Predictable income for you, a lower per-tan price for them.

Keep discounts modest, around 10% to 15%. Deeper cuts teach clients to wait for deals.

Paperwork you need before the first tan

Every client should complete an intake and consent form before their first session. It should cover skin sensitivities, allergies, medications, pregnancy, and an acknowledgment of the prep and aftercare instructions. Give every client a prep and aftercare card so they know not to shower too early or apply lotion before their appointment. Fewer streaky tans means fewer complaints.
