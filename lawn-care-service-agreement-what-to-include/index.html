Lawn Care Service Agreement: What to Include (With Checklist)

Updated November 12, 2026 · 3 min read

A handshake works until the first rainy week, the first late payment or the first broken sprinkler head. A short written service agreement settles those questions before they turn into arguments, and it makes your business look serious. Here is what a good lawn care agreement covers.

This is general information, not legal advice. Rules vary by state, so have a local professional review your final agreement.

1. The parties and the property

List your business name and contact details, the customer's name and contact details, and the service address. If the customer is a landlord or property manager, note who approves extra work and who pays.

2. Scope of services

Spell out exactly what each visit includes. For example:

  • Mow all turf areas at a set height
  • String trim around obstacles, fences and beds
  • Edge sidewalks, driveway and curbs
  • Blow clippings off hard surfaces

Also list what is not included, such as bagging clippings, leaf removal, weeding beds or hedge trimming. Those can be offered as add-ons with their own prices.

3. Frequency and season

State how often you'll visit (weekly, every two weeks) and the season dates, for example "approximately April 1 through October 31." Note that frequency may change during dry spells or heavy growth periods, and how you'll communicate that.

4. Price and payment terms

  • Price per visit, or a seasonal price split into monthly payments
  • When invoices are sent and when payment is due
  • Accepted payment methods
  • Late fees, if any, and when they apply
  • What happens if payment is more than a set number of days late (for example, service paused)

5. Weather and rescheduling

This is the section that prevents the most arguments. Cover:

  • How rain delays work (for example, service moves to the next dry day)
  • Whether a skipped week due to weather is billed
  • Drought policy: whether you reduce visits when grass stops growing

6. Access and property conditions

  • Gate codes, locked gates and pets in the yard
  • The customer's responsibility to clear toys, hoses, dog waste and debris before service
  • What happens if you can't access the yard (trip charge or skipped visit)

7. Damage and liability

Explain how you handle damage you cause, and what you are not responsible for, such as hidden sprinkler heads, invisible dog fences, or items left in the grass that were not marked. Mention that you carry liability insurance if you do.

8. Changes and extra work

Extra work outside the agreed scope is quoted separately and approved before you do it, in writing or by text.

9. Cancellation and term

How either side can end the agreement and how much notice is required (for example, 14 days in writing). For seasonal contracts billed monthly, explain how the final balance is settled if the customer cancels mid-season.

10. Signatures and date

Both parties sign and date. A digital signature or a photo of a signed page is fine for most small businesses.
