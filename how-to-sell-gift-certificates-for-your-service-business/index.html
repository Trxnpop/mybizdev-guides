How to Sell Gift Certificates for Your Service Business This Christmas

Updated November 25, 2026 · 3 min read

Gift certificates are the easiest extra money a small service business can make in December. Your clients are already looking for gifts, and a cleaning, a set of lashes or a massage is easy to give. You get paid now, often in a slow week, and the person who receives it is a brand new client. Here is a simple plan to start selling them this week.

1. Decide what you'll sell

Offer two kinds of certificates:

  • Dollar amounts: $25, $50, $75 and $100 are easy to choose and work for any service.
  • Specific services: "one deep clean," "a full set of lashes," "a 60-minute massage." These feel more like a real gift and are easier to redeem.

Make the most popular amount or service the obvious choice. In most businesses, it's the price of one typical visit.

2. Add a reason to buy now

You don't need to discount your services to sell gift certificates. Small bonuses work well:

  • Buy $100, get a $15 bonus card for the buyer to use themselves
  • Free add-on with a service certificate, such as nail art, a brow tint or an inside-the-fridge clean
  • Black Friday to Cyber Monday only bonus offers, which give people a reason to act this weekend

Keep the bonus small enough that it's still profitable if every certificate gets used.

3. Make it easy to buy

People buy gift certificates on impulse, so remove every step you can:

  • Take payment by card, Venmo, Cash App or your booking app
  • Offer an email version that can be sent the same day, for last-minute shoppers
  • Keep printed certificates at your front desk or in your car, in envelopes, ready to hand over

4. Tell people (more than once)

Most clients don't know you sell gift certificates unless you say so. A simple December schedule:

  • Black Friday weekend: announce gift certificates on Instagram and Facebook, and in a text to your client list
  • Every appointment: mention it at checkout and put a small sign by your payment area
  • December 15 to 23: post a "last-minute gift" reminder. This is when most certificates are sold
  • December 24: remind people that email certificates can be sent instantly

5. Put the rules on every certificate

A few details printed on each certificate prevent confusion later:

  • Certificate number, so you can track it
  • Amount or service, and the client's name
  • Your business name, phone and website, and how to book
  • Expiration date, if you use one. Many states limit or ban expiration dates on gift certificates, so check your state's rules before you print one
  • Whether it can be exchanged for cash (usually not)

6. Track every certificate

Keep a simple log: certificate number, buyer, recipient, amount, date sold and date redeemed. It takes a minute per sale and stops double redemptions. It also shows how many new clients your certificates brought in.

When a gift recipient comes in, treat the visit like an audition for a long-term client. Book their next appointment before they leave.
