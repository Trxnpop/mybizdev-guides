Lash Extension Consent Form: What to Include

Updated November 12, 2026 · 3 min read

Every lash artist needs a signed consent form before the first appointment. It records that the client understood the service and its risks, shares the health information you need to work safely, and protects you if a client later has a reaction. Here's what a good consent form covers.

This is general information, not legal or medical advice. Licensing and record rules vary by state and country, so check with your licensing board and insurer.

Intake: the health questions to ask first

Collect this before the client lies down, ideally on an intake form completed at booking or on arrival:

  • Allergies, especially to adhesives, cyanoacrylate, latex, tape or cosmetics
  • Recent eye surgery, infections, styes or conjunctivitis
  • Eye conditions such as dry eye, blepharitis or glaucoma
  • Contact lens use
  • Use of lash growth serums or medications that affect hair growth
  • Recent chemotherapy or conditions that affect natural lashes
  • Pregnancy, if relevant to your policies or products
  • Sensitivity to fumes or claustrophobia
  • Previous reactions to lash services
  • Whether they're wearing makeup or have had a lash lift or tint recently

If a client answers yes to something that concerns you, it's appropriate to delay the service or ask them to check with a doctor.

What the consent and release should include

1. Description of the service

Name the service (classic, hybrid or volume extensions, lash lift, brow lamination, tint) and explain briefly what it involves: adhesive near the eye, eyes closed for an extended time.

2. Known risks

Explain possible risks in plain language: eye irritation, redness, allergic reaction to adhesive or tint, discomfort, and temporary damage to natural lashes if aftercare isn't followed.

3. Patch test

Whether you offer or require a patch test before the first full service, and what the client is accepting if they decline it.

4. Client responsibilities

The client confirms the health information is accurate, agrees to follow aftercare instructions, and agrees to tell you right away if anything feels wrong during the service.

5. Reaction protocol

What the client should do if they have a reaction after leaving, such as contact you and seek medical attention, and your policy on removal.

6. Results disclaimer

Lash retention varies with natural lash cycles, oil, sleep habits and aftercare. You can't guarantee how long extensions last.

7. Photo release

Optional permission to photograph the client's lashes for your portfolio and social media, with a box to opt out.

8. Signature, date and annual update

Client signature and date, plus yours. Ask returning clients to update their health information regularly and whenever something changes.

Keep a service record for every client

Alongside the consent form, keep a record of each appointment: the lash map, curl, length, thickness, adhesive used and any notes. If a client reacts, you can see exactly what you used. It also makes fills faster because you aren't guessing what you did last time.

Pair it with clear studio policies

Most disputes come from late arrivals, cancellations and refunds, not reactions. Put your deposit, lateness and cancellation rules in a written studio policy and have clients acknowledge it at booking.
