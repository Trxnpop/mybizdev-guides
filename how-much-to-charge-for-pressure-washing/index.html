How Much to Charge for Pressure Washing (Per Square Foot Guide)

Updated November 13, 2026 · 3 min read

Pressure washing is priced by area more than almost any other service. Once you know your square-foot rates and minimum charge, you can quote most jobs from a satellite photo and a few measurements. Here's how to set those numbers.

Price flatwork and siding by square foot

Flatwork (driveways, sidewalks, patios, pool decks) and house washing (siding) are usually priced per square foot. Example ranges seen in many US markets (check local competitors):

Surface Method Example rate
Concrete driveway, sidewalk, patio Surface cleaner $0.10 to $0.35 per sq ft
House wash (vinyl siding) Soft wash $0.15 to $0.40 per sq ft of siding
Wood deck Low pressure, cleaner $0.50 to $1.50 per sq ft
Fence Per linear foot or sq ft Varies by height and material
Roof soft wash Low pressure, chemicals Often priced per sq ft of roof, higher due to risk

Many companies also price house washes by the home's square footage or number of stories, which is simpler to quote.

Set a minimum charge

Setup, travel, water and chemicals cost the same whether you clean 200 or 2,000 square feet. A minimum charge, often somewhere around $150 to $250, keeps small jobs worth doing.

Charge for stains and conditions

  • Rust, oil and battery acid stains: priced per stain or by area, with a note that some stains may not fully come out
  • Heavy mildew, algae or moss
  • Gum removal on commercial sidewalks
  • Second stories and steep pitches: extra for ladders, time and risk
  • Sealing concrete or wood after cleaning, priced separately

Bundle services to raise the ticket

Customers who book a house wash often want the driveway done too. Offer bundles, such as house wash plus driveway plus sidewalk, at a small discount compared to booking each separately. Bundles raise your average job size and fill a day with fewer driving stops.

Do a pre-job walkaround

Before you start, walk the property with the customer and note existing damage: oxidized siding, loose boards, cracked concrete, failing paint, plants that need protection and outlets or lights that need covering. Record it in writing with photos. It protects you from blame for pre-existing problems.

Keep a chemical log

Soft washing uses chemicals like sodium hypochlorite and surfactants. Recording what you mixed, the strength and where you used it is good practice for safety, consistency and any questions about plants or surfaces later. Check your local rules on runoff.
