How Much to Charge for Pet Sitting and Dog Walking

Updated November 12, 2026 · 3 min read

New pet sitters usually underprice because they love animals and feel awkward asking for money. But pet care is skilled, trusted work: you are responsible for someone's family member and their home. Here is how to set rates that reflect that and still win clients.

Start by checking your local market

Rates vary a lot between a small town and a big city. Before setting yours:

  • Look at the rates independent sitters list in local Facebook groups and on their websites.
  • Check what sitters charge on the big booking apps in your zip code. Remember those apps take a cut, so independent sitters often price at or slightly below the app price and still earn more.
  • Decide where you want to sit: budget, middle or premium. Experience, certifications and great reviews justify the top of the range.

Example rate ranges

These are rough ranges seen across many US markets, to show how services are usually structured. Your local rates may be higher or lower.

Service Typical length Example range
Drop-in visit (feed, potty, play) 20 to 30 min $18 to $30
Longer drop-in 45 to 60 min $25 to $40

Dog walk, solo 60 min $30 to $45
Group dog walk 30 to 60 min Less per dog than solo
Overnight stay in client's home About 12 hours $50 to $100+
Cat care visit 20 to 30 min $18 to $28

Price the extras clearly

Clients accept add-on fees when they see them on a rate sheet. Common ones:

  • Additional pets: $5 to $10 per extra pet per visit
  • Holidays: 1.25x to 1.5x for major holidays
  • Last-minute bookings: a same-day or next-day fee
  • Medication: small fee for pills, more for injections such as insulin
  • Puppies and seniors: extra time and care may justify a higher rate
  • Key pickup and meet-and-greet: free for booked clients, or a fee if they don't book
  • Distance: a travel fee outside your core area

Work out what you earn per hour

A 30-minute visit isn't 30 minutes of work. Add travel time on both sides, texting updates and scheduling. If a $25 visit takes 50 minutes door to door, you are earning $30 an hour before gas and taxes. Group visits by neighborhood to raise your real hourly rate.

Packages and recurring discounts

Daily dog walking clients are the most valuable kind: predictable income on a set route. Reward them with packages, such as a small discount for five walks a week paid weekly or monthly in advance. Avoid discounting one-off visits; those take more coordination per dollar.

Policies that protect your income

Put these in your service agreement:

  • Cancellation policy. For example, full refund with 48 hours' notice, 50% inside 48 hours, and stricter rules for holidays.
  • Deposits for holiday and long bookings.
  • Payment timing. Many sitters require payment before the booking starts.
  • Late return fee if the owner comes back later than planned.

Raise your rates as you grow

When your calendar is full and you're turning people away, it's time to raise prices. Give current clients 30 days' notice, and consider keeping loyal clients at the old rate for a few months.
