How to Start a Mobile Car Detailing Business

Updated November 13, 2026 · 3 min read

Mobile detailing lets you start without a shop: you bring the wash to the customer's driveway or office parking lot. Startup costs are modest, demand is year-round in many climates, and happy customers book again and again. Here's how to set it up.

1. Build your mobile rig

You need a vehicle that can carry everything, plus:

  • Water: a tank (many detailers carry 50 to 100+ gallons) or waterless and rinseless products
  • Power: a generator or inverter for vacuums, extractors and polishers
  • Pressure washer and foam cannon
  • Vacuum and carpet extractor
  • Dual-action polisher for paint correction
  • Microfiber towels, wash mitts, brushes and detailing swabs
  • Chemicals: car shampoo, wheel cleaner, degreaser, interior cleaner, glass cleaner, dressings, waxes or sealants
  • Organized storage so setup and teardown are fast

Start with the essentials and upgrade as you add services.

2. Handle licenses and insurance

Check local business license requirements and any rules about washing vehicles at customers' locations, including wash water runoff. Register your business name, get an EIN and open a business bank account. Get general liability insurance and garagekeepers coverage, which covers damage to customer vehicles in your care.

3. Learn the craft

Paint correction and ceramic coatings can damage paint if done wrong. Practice on your own cars and friends' cars, take a training course, and only offer advanced services once you're confident.

4. Build your service menu

Offer three clear packages priced by vehicle size, plus condition fees and add-ons. See mobile detailing prices for an example menu.

5. Inspect every vehicle

Before you touch a car, walk around it with the customer and record existing damage with photos. Have them sign the inspection form. It's the best protection you have against damage disputes.

6. Use a checklist

A detail checklist keeps every job consistent and makes training a helper easy. Work in the same order every time: wheels and tires first, then wash, decontaminate, dry, protect, then the interior from top to bottom, and finish with glass and a final walkaround.

7. Sell memberships

Offer a maintenance plan after the first full detail, such as a monthly or biweekly wash at a set price. Memberships smooth out your income and make scheduling easier.

8. Get customers

  • Google Business Profile with before-and-after photos and videos
  • Instagram and TikTok: transformation videos perform well
  • Office parks and apartment complexes: detail several cars in one stop
  • Dealerships and car lots for wholesale work
  • Referral rewards for customers who send friends

9. Track your money

Log every job, payment and expense (chemicals, fuel, equipment). Track your profit per hour by package, so you know which services to promote.

Launch checklist

  • Vehicle, water and power setup
  • Core equipment and chemicals
  • License, EIN, bank account and insurance (including garagekeepers)
  • Service menu priced by vehicle size
  • Vehicle inspection form, checklist and agreement
  • Membership offer
  • Google Business Profile and social media
