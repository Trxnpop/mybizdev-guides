How Much to Charge for Christmas Light Installation (Per Foot and Per House)

Updated November 19, 2026 · 3 min read

Christmas light installation is one of the best short-season side businesses there is. The work is concentrated into about eight weeks, customers book early, and a good crew can install several homes a day. The catch is pricing: quote too low and you spend December on a ladder for nothing, quote by gut feel and every job turns into a negotiation. Here is a simple way to price any house.

Price by the roofline foot

Most lighting companies quote by the linear foot of roofline (gutters, eaves and peaks) and then add line items for everything else. It is easy to measure, easy to explain, and it scales with the real work.

To measure, walk the house with a measuring wheel along the gutter line, or count the gutter sections and downspouts on a satellite view and confirm on site. Write down each run separately (front, garage, side peaks) so you can quote "front only" and "full house" options.

Example starting rates

These are common starting points in many US markets. Your area may be higher or lower, so check what local companies advertise before you set your own.

Item Typical starting price
Roofline lights, single story (customer-owned lights) $2 to $4 per foot
Roofline lights, single story (you supply lights) $4 to $8 per foot
Second story or steep roof add 25% to 50%
Small tree or bush, wrapped $25 to $75 each
Large tree trunk and branch wrap $100 to $300+ each
Wreath or garland hung $25 to $60 each
Pathway or stake lights $3 to $6 per stake
Minimum job $250 to $400

A minimum charge matters more than any rate on this list. Driving to a house, setting up ladders and testing circuits takes the same time whether you hang 20 feet or 120 feet.

Customer-owned lights or leased lights

You have two business models, and you can offer both:

  • Customer-owned lights: the homeowner already has lights (or buys them). You charge labor only. Lower ticket, but you will spend time untangling old strands and replacing dead bulbs, so charge for repairs or replacement strands separately.
  • Leased lights: you buy commercial-grade lights cut to fit the house, install them, take them down and store them. The customer pays one price each year. Higher ticket, fewer service calls, and the lights you bought in year one keep earning in year two and three.

Most established installers push the leased model because the first-year price covers the lights and the following years are mostly profit.

Do not forget takedown and storage

Quote installation, takedown and storage as one package so the customer is not surprised in January. A common approach is to include takedown in the price and charge a storage fee (often $50 to $150 a season) if you keep their lights. Put the takedown window in writing, for example "lights removed between January 2 and January 20."

A simple formula for any house

  1. Measure roofline feet and multiply by your per-foot rate.
  2. Add the story or steep-roof adjustment.
  3. Add trees, bushes, wreaths and stakes as line items.
  4. Add takedown and storage.
  5. Compare to your minimum and use whichever is higher.

Example: 140 feet of single-story roofline with your lights at $5/ft is $700. Two wrapped bushes at $40 each add $80, and a wreath adds $40. Takedown is included and storage is $75. The quote is $895.

Get it in writing

Holiday lighting has a few risks other services don't: roof access, gutters and shingles, timers and outdoor outlets, and weather delays. A short installation agreement should cover:

  • What is being installed and where (attach your design sketch or photos)
  • Who owns the lights and who replaces failed strands during the season
  • Install date window and takedown date window
  • Deposit (many installers take 50% to book) and payment terms
  • That you are not responsible for pre-existing electrical problems or tripped GFCI outlets

Book early and rebook every year

The busiest installers start booking in September and are full by Thanksgiving. Once you finish a job, offer next year's install at this year's price if they rebook before takedown. It is the cheapest marketing you will ever do.
