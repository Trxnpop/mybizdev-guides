How to Start a Pressure Washing Business

Updated November 13, 2026 · 3 min read

Pressure washing is a popular side hustle for good reason: the results are dramatic, customers love before-and-after photos, and jobs can be quoted from a satellite view. It's also easy to damage property if you don't know what you're doing. Here's how to start right.

1. Learn pressure washing vs soft washing

  • Pressure washing uses high pressure to clean hard surfaces like concrete.
  • Soft washing uses low pressure and cleaning solutions for siding, roofs and delicate surfaces.

Using high pressure on siding, wood or roofs can strip paint, etch wood, force water behind siding and void roof warranties. Learn which method fits each surface before taking paid jobs.

2. Buy your equipment

  • A gas pressure washer rated for commercial use (flow rate in gallons per minute matters as much as PSI)
  • Surface cleaner for flatwork
  • Soft wash setup: pump, chemical tank and hoses (many start with a downstream injector)
  • Nozzles, extension wands, hoses and reels
  • Water tank if you can't rely on customer water supply
  • Safety gear: eye protection, gloves, non-slip boots and hearing protection
  • A truck or trailer to carry it all

3. Get insured and licensed

Check local business license requirements and any rules about wash water runoff. Register your business name, get an EIN, open a business bank account, and buy general liability insurance. Ask specifically about coverage for property damage and chemical use.

4. Set your prices

Price flatwork and siding by square foot, set a minimum charge, and add fees for stains and second stories. See how much to charge for pressure washing for example rates.

5. Protect yourself on every job

  • Pre-job walkaround: note existing damage with photos and have the customer sign
  • Service agreement: what's included, weather rescheduling, water access, payment terms and limits on stain results
  • Chemical and job log: what you mixed and used where
  • Cover plants, outlets and lights, and pre-wet landscaping when soft washing

6. Find customers

  • Before-and-after photos on your Google Business Profile, Facebook and Nextdoor
  • Door hangers in neighborhoods where you just cleaned a driveway (neighbors notice)
  • HOAs and property managers for community sidewalks and buildings
  • Realtors for pre-listing curb appeal
  • Restaurants and storefronts for regular sidewalk and dumpster pad cleaning
  • Seasonal reminders: spring and pre-holiday are busy

7. Build recurring work

Offer annual or twice-yearly house wash plans, monthly commercial sidewalk cleaning and bundles with window cleaning or gutter cleaning.

8. Track your profit

Log each job, payment and expense: fuel, chemicals, equipment maintenance and repairs. Track profit per hour by job type so you know which work to chase.

Launch checklist

  • Learn pressure vs soft washing for each surface
  • Commercial-grade equipment and safety gear
  • License, EIN, bank account and insurance
  • Square-foot price list and minimum charge
  • Pre-job walkaround, agreement and chemical log
  • Google Business Profile with photos
