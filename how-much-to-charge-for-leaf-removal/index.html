How Much to Charge for Leaf Removal (Per Hour, Per Bag and Per Yard)

Updated November 25, 2026 · 3 min read

Fall cleanups are some of the best-paying jobs a lawn care business does all year. Homeowners want the leaves gone before the snow, the work is easy to sell door to door, and a two-person crew can clear several yards a day. The tricky part is quoting: a yard with one maple and a yard under six oaks are completely different jobs. Here is how to price leaf removal so every job pays.

The three ways to charge

By the hour. Simple and safe when you can't tell how deep the leaves are. Charge per person-hour so a two-person crew bills double.

By yard size. A flat price for small, medium and large yards, adjusted for how many trees there are. Customers like knowing the price up front.

By the bag or by the load. Common for haul-away work. You charge for every lawn bag or every truck or trailer load you remove.

Many companies quote a flat price from a quick look at the yard, but they figure that price from an hourly target behind the scenes.

Example starting rates

These are common starting points in many US markets. Check what local lawn services charge before setting your own.

Service Typical starting price
Leaf removal, per person-hour $45 to $75
Small yard (under 1/4 acre), light leaves $150 to $300
Medium yard (1/4 to 1/2 acre) $250 to $500
Large yard or heavy tree cover $450 to $900+
Per lawn bag filled $3 to $6 per bag
Haul-away, per pickup or trailer load $75 to $200
Gutter clearing add-on $75 to $200
Bed cleanout and cutting back perennials $50 to $150 add-on
Minimum job $125 to $200

What changes the price

  • Tree cover. Count the big trees. More trees means deeper, wetter leaves and more trips.
  • Wet or matted leaves. Leaves that sat through a few rains weigh much more and take longer to move.
  • Beds and fence lines. Leaves packed into shrubs, rock beds and along fences have to be cleaned by hand.
  • Disposal. Blowing leaves to the curb for city pickup is much faster than bagging and hauling. Price disposal fees into haul-away jobs.
  • Equipment. A backpack blower and tarps are fine for small yards. Big properties go much faster with a mower bagger, a leaf vacuum or a walk-behind blower.

A simple quote formula

  1. Estimate crew hours from the yard size and tree cover.
  2. Multiply by your hourly rate per person.
  3. Add disposal: bags, loads or dump fees.
  4. Add extras like gutters and bed cleanouts.
  5. Use your minimum if the total is lower.

Example: a medium yard with four large trees will take two people about 2.5 hours. At $55 per person-hour that is $275. Twelve bags at $4 each adds $48, and clearing the gutters adds $100. The quote is $423.

Sell the second visit up front

Leaves don't all fall at once. Offer a two-visit fall package: one cleanup mid-season and a final one after the last leaves drop, at a small discount if they book both. You get two jobs from one sale, and the customer gets a clean yard going into winter.

Fall cleanup customers are also the easiest people to sign up for next year's mowing. Give every cleanup customer a quote for a spring cleanup and weekly mowing before you leave. Our guide on how much to charge for lawn mowing covers that pricing.

Put it in writing

A short written estimate prevents most "I thought that was included" arguments. List the areas you will clear, how leaves will be disposed of, the extras, and the price. Note that the price assumes normal leaf depth, so a big storm the night before may change it.
