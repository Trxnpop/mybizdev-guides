How to Start a Painting Business

Updated November 13, 2026 · 3 min read

Painting is one of the most approachable trades to turn into a business. The tools are affordable, demand is steady, and a fresh coat of paint is one of the most popular home improvements. What separates profitable painters from busy, broke ones is how they estimate, contract and run jobs.

1. Check licensing rules

Some states require a contractor's license for painting, especially above a certain job value; others only need a local business license. If you work on homes built before 1978, US federal rules require lead-safe certification (EPA RRP) for jobs that disturb painted surfaces. Check your state and local requirements before you bid.

2. Set up the business

Register your business name, get an EIN, open a business bank account and buy general liability insurance. If you hire help, look into workers' compensation requirements. Many homeowners will ask for proof of insurance before hiring you.

3. Buy the right equipment

Start with quality brushes and rollers, extension poles, ladders (step and extension), drop cloths and plastic, painter's tape, caulk guns, sanders and scrapers, patching tools, a paint sprayer once you take on larger jobs, and a vehicle that can carry it all. Good tools make faster, cleaner work.

4. Choose your niche

  • Interior residential: year-round work, smaller jobs
  • Exterior residential: bigger tickets, seasonal in many climates
  • Cabinet painting: high-value specialty work
  • Rental turnovers: steady work from landlords and property managers

5. Price every job the same way

Measure, estimate hours with production rates, add materials, overhead and profit. Our guide on how to price a painting job walks through it with an example.

6. Use contracts and deposits

A written painting contract should cover the scope, surfaces, number of coats, paint brand and colors, prep included, schedule, payment terms, deposit, change orders, cleanup and warranty. Many painters take a deposit to schedule the job and to buy materials. Check whether your state limits deposit amounts.

7. Get colors right

Wrong colors on the wrong walls are expensive to fix. Use a color and paint schedule that lists each room or surface, the brand, color name and code, sheen and number of coats, and have the client sign it.

8. Finish with a walkthrough

Walk the job with the client at the end, fix touch-ups on the spot and have them sign off. It prevents callbacks and is the perfect moment to ask for a review.

9. Find your first jobs

  • Google Business Profile with project photos
  • Nextdoor and local Facebook groups
  • Realtors for pre-listing and move-in painting
  • Property managers for rental turnovers
  • Paint store staff who hear about jobs every day
  • Yard signs at every exterior job (with permission)

Launch checklist

  • Check licensing and lead-safe requirements
  • Register, EIN, bank account and insurance
  • Core tools and ladders
  • Estimate method and price list
  • Painting contract and deposit policy
  • Color schedule and completion walkthrough forms
  • Google Business Profile and photos
