How to Price Lash Extensions and Fills as a New Lash Tech

Updated November 12, 2026 · 3 min read

New lash techs face a tough choice: charge too little and you're booked solid but exhausted and barely profitable; charge too much and your chair stays empty. The answer is to price from your real numbers, then adjust to your market and skill. Here's how.

Step 1: Know your cost per hour

Add up what it costs to run your chair each month:

  • Suite or booth rent
  • Lash trays, adhesive, primer, tape, pads, tweezers replacement and disposables
  • Insurance and license renewals
  • Booking software and card processing fees
  • Continuing education
  • Marketing

Divide by the hours you're actually booked, not the hours you're available. If your costs are $1,200 a month and you lash 80 hours, your overhead is $15 an hour before you pay yourself.

Step 2: Decide your hourly target

Add your desired pay plus around 15% for self-employment tax (US) to your overhead. A tech who wants $35 an hour take-home with $15 an hour overhead needs to earn roughly $55 to $60 per service hour.

Step 3: Price each service by time

Now price each service by how long it takes you. New techs are slower, so start with your real times and raise prices as your speed and skill improve.

Service Typical time for a newer tech How to price
Classic full set 2 to 2.5 hours Time × hourly target
Hybrid full set 2.5 to 3 hours Time × target, plus materials
Volume full set 2.5 to 3.5 hours Higher tier for skill and fans
Fill (2 to 3 weeks) 45 to 75 minutes Priced by time, with a minimum retention rule
Lash lift and tint 60 to 75 minutes Flat price
Brow lamination 45 to 60 minutes Flat price, add tint or wax
Removal 15 to 30 minutes Small flat fee or free with a new set

Rates vary hugely by region and experience. Check five or six techs near you, note their prices and experience level, and position yourself honestly. Many new techs start just below established artists and raise prices as their portfolio grows.

Step 4: Set fill rules that protect you

Fills only work financially if they are actually fills. Common policies:

  • Fills priced by how many weeks since the last appointment (for example, 2-week and 3-week fills)
  • A minimum percentage of extensions remaining, otherwise it's charged as a new full set
  • Foreign fill fee for another artist's work, since it takes longer

Step 5: Use deposits and a cancellation policy

No-shows hurt more in lash work than almost anywhere else because each appointment takes hours. A non-refundable deposit at booking (often applied to the service) and a clear late and cancellation policy keep your schedule full. Put the rules in writing and have clients acknowledge them.

Step 6: Raise prices with your skill

Natural moments to raise prices: when you finish an advanced course, when you're booked out more than two weeks, or once a year. Give clients notice, and consider honoring the old price for loyal regulars for a short time.

Common lash pricing mistakes

  • Copying the cheapest tech in town. It attracts price-shoppers who rarely rebook.
  • Ignoring your real time. If a "2-hour" set takes you 3, your hourly rate is a third lower than you think.
  • Free fixes forever. Offer a short, clear window for retention fixes, tied to aftercare being followed.
  • No deposit. One no-show can wipe out an afternoon.
