5 Service Businesses You Can Start With Little Money

Updated November 12, 2026 · 4 min read

Service businesses are the fastest way to start earning on your own. There's no inventory, no storefront and often no special degree. You trade skill and reliability for money, and good clients come back every week. Here are five of the most popular options, how they compare and how to pick one.

At a glance

Startup costs and earnings are rough estimates and vary by area, but they show how the options compare.

1. House cleaning

Cleaning is the classic low-cost start. You can begin with supplies under a few hundred dollars and fill a weekly schedule with recurring clients. Profit depends almost entirely on pricing well and keeping drive time low.

Read: How to start a house cleaning business · How to price house cleaning jobs

2. Lawn care

Lawn care rewards hustle and tight routes. Equipment costs more than cleaning, but per-hour earnings can be strong once your route is dense. In cold climates, plan off-season services like leaf cleanup or snow removal.

Read: How to start a lawn care business · How much to charge for lawn mowing

3. Pet sitting and dog walking

The cheapest start of all. Daily dog walks create very steady income, and holiday sitting can be lucrative. The work is built on trust, so professional forms and fast updates matter a lot.

Read: How to start a pet sitting business · How much to charge for pet sitting

4. Handyman

If you already own tools, a handyman business can start almost immediately. Hourly rates are among the highest on this list, but check your state's limits on job size and licensed trades.

Read: How to start a handyman business · How to price handyman jobs

5. Lash and brow

Lash work needs training and, in most places, a license, so it's a longer runway. In exchange, clients rebook every few weeks and a full book can be very profitable.

What they all have in common

Whichever business you choose, the same foundations decide whether it works:

  1. Price from your real costs, not from the cheapest competitor.
  2. Use written agreements so cancellations, payment and damage are settled before they happen.
  3. Collect client details up front with an intake form.
  4. Invoice every job and know who hasn't paid.
  5. Track income and expenses from day one so taxes aren't a shock.
  6. Ask for reviews after every great job.

How to choose

  • Want to start this week with almost no money? Pet sitting or cleaning.
  • Prefer working outside and have a truck or trailer? Lawn care.
  • Already handy with a garage full of tools? Handyman.
  • Looking for a skilled, creative career and willing to train? Lash and brow.
  • Not sure, or want to run more than one? Many people combine them, such as cleaning and pet sitting, or lawn care and handyman.
