Virtual Assistant Rates for Beginners (Hourly, Packages and Retainers)

Updated November 19, 2026 · 3 min read

Starting a virtual assistant business takes little more than a laptop, reliable internet and skills you probably already use every day. The hardest part for most new VAs is deciding what to charge. Charge too little and you'll be stuck working long hours for clients who don't value you. Here is how to set beginner rates and grow them quickly.

Hourly rate: where most beginners start

Hourly billing is easy to explain and easy to track. To set your first rate, consider:

  • Your skills: general admin tasks pay less than specialized work like bookkeeping, podcast editing or running ads.
  • Your experience: past office, customer service or project work counts, even if it wasn't called "VA."
  • Your costs: self-employment tax, software, equipment and unpaid time spent on sales and admin.

Example rates by type of work

Rates vary widely by experience, niche and who your clients are. Common starting points for US-based VAs:

Type of work Typical beginner rate
General admin (email, calendar, data entry) $20 to $35 per hour
Customer service and inbox support $22 to $35 per hour
Social media scheduling $25 to $40 per hour
Bookkeeping support $30 to $50 per hour
Online business management, launches $40 to $75+ per hour
Rush work (same day) add 25% to 50%

Your rate should go up as you gain experience and testimonials. Many VAs raise rates for new clients every few months during their first year.

Move to monthly retainers

Retainers are a set number of hours each month for a flat fee, paid in advance. They're better for you (predictable income) and often better for clients (a reserved block of your time).

Example: 20 hours a month at $30 per hour is $600. Offer it as a retainer at $575 a month, paid on the 1st, with unused hours not rolling over (or rolling over for one month only).

Common package sizes are 10, 20 and 40 hours per month. Add a fixed rate for hours beyond the package.

Track your time even on retainers

Even if you charge flat monthly fees, track every task and how long it takes. You'll find out which clients are actually profitable, and you'll have data to justify a raise or a bigger package. Send a short monthly report showing what you completed. Clients who can see your work are much less likely to cancel.

What goes in a VA contract

Before you start, both of you should sign an agreement that covers:

  • Services included, package hours and the rate for extra hours
  • Payment terms (retainers paid in advance) and late fees
  • Working hours and response times
  • Confidentiality and handling of passwords and client data
  • How either side can end the agreement (30 days notice is common)

Use a password manager or the client's team accounts instead of having passwords sent by email.
