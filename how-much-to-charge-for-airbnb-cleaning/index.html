How Much to Charge for Airbnb Cleaning (Turnover Pricing Guide)

Updated November 13, 2026 · 3 min read

Turnover cleaning is not the same as house cleaning. You are resetting a home for paying guests on a deadline, often between an 11 a.m. checkout and a 3 p.m. check-in. Hosts care about speed, consistency and photos that prove the place is ready. That is worth more than a standard clean, and your prices should show it.

What a turnover includes

Before you price, define the job. A typical turnover covers:

  • Stripping and remaking every bed, and starting laundry
  • Cleaning bathrooms and the kitchen to "hotel standard"
  • Emptying all trash and the dishwasher
  • Vacuuming and mopping every floor
  • Restocking toiletries, coffee, paper goods and cleaning supplies
  • Checking for damage, missing items and anything left behind
  • Staging: towels folded, pillows arranged, lights and thermostat set
  • Photos and a quick report to the host

Write this down as your standard turnover checklist. Everything else is an add-on.

Price by bedrooms and bathrooms

Most turnover cleaners use a flat fee per property, built from bedrooms and bathrooms. Example structure (adjust for your market):

Property Typical time (one cleaner) Example turnover price
Studio or 1 bed, 1 bath 1.5 to 2 hours $70 to $110
2 bed, 1 to 2 bath 2 to 3 hours $95 to $150
3 bed, 2 bath 3 to 4 hours $130 to $200
4+ bed, 3+ bath 4+ hours $180 and up, or quote by the hour

Check what local cleaners charge in host Facebook groups, and ask a couple of hosts what they pay now. Rates vary a lot between a beach town and a small city.

Charge separately for linens and laundry

Laundry is where turnover cleaners lose the most money. Decide up front:

  • On-site laundry (washer and dryer at the property): add a per-bed or per-load fee, because you may need to wait or come back.
  • Off-site laundry (you take linens home or to a laundromat): charge per set, typically enough to cover machines, detergent and your time.
  • Host-supplied extra sets make turnovers faster. Recommend hosts keep two or three full sets per bed.

Add-ons and fees to put on your price list

  • Same-day or short-notice turnovers: a premium, often 25% to 50%
  • Restocking: a flat fee plus the cost of supplies, or included if the host supplies a closet of stock
  • Deep clean: quarterly or between seasons, priced like a residential deep clean
  • Hot tub, grill or patio cleaning
  • Excess mess fee: for parties, pets or heavy cleanup, with photos as evidence
  • Damage and lost item reporting: usually included, but documented every time

Volume discounts for hosts with several properties

Co-hosts and property managers with several listings are your best clients. A small per-turnover discount for guaranteed monthly volume (for example, 20+ turnovers a month) keeps your schedule full. Only discount once the volume is real.

Protect yourself with an agreement

Turnover work comes with specific risks: last-minute cancellations, guests who don't leave on time and damage disputes. Your agreement should cover cancellation fees, what happens when a guest checks out late, how you report damage, who supplies linens and products, and payment terms (many cleaners invoice weekly or monthly).
