How Much to Charge for Gutter Cleaning (Per Foot and Per Story)

Updated November 19, 2026 · 3 min read

Gutter cleaning is a simple service with a lot of repeat demand. Most homes need it at least once a year and many need it every spring and fall. The equipment cost is low, but the risk (ladders and rooflines) is real, so your pricing has to cover your time, your safety gear and your insurance. Here is how to quote any house quickly.

Price by linear foot, then adjust for height

The most common method is price per linear foot of gutter, adjusted for the number of stories. Measure the gutter runs on site or from a satellite view, then confirm on the walkaround.

Some companies prefer a flat price per house size. That works once you know your market, but per-foot pricing is easier to defend when a customer asks why their neighbor paid less.

Example starting rates

These are common starting points in many US markets. Check local competitors before you settle on your own numbers.

Item Typical starting price
Single story, per linear foot $1.00 to $1.50
Two story, per linear foot $1.50 to $2.25
Three story or steep roof $2.25 to $3.00+
Downspout flush (each) $10 to $25
Heavy clog or plant growth add $25 to $75
Gutter guard cleaning (remove and replace) add 30% to 50%
Minimum job $100 to $150

Rule of thumb: many single-story homes have 120 to 200 feet of gutter, so you will often land between $150 and $250 for a basic clean.

What changes the price

  • Height and roof pitch. Second and third stories mean longer ladders, more setup time and more risk.
  • Gutter guards. Guards keep out leaves, but cleaning them means removing, cleaning and reinstalling sections.
  • Debris level. A gutter that hasn't been cleaned in five years with seedlings growing in it is a different job from a maintenance clean.
  • Access. Fences, landscaping beds, decks and steep slopes slow down ladder moves.
  • Haul-away. Bagging and removing debris instead of leaving it in the yard waste bin.

A simple formula

  1. Linear feet × your per-foot rate for the number of stories.
  2. Add downspouts that need flushing.
  3. Add guards, heavy debris or haul-away.
  4. Use your minimum if the total comes out lower.

Example: 160 feet on a two-story home at $1.75/ft is $280. Flushing four downspouts at $15 each adds $60. The quote is $340.

Sell plans, not one-time cleanings

Leaves fall every year, so offer a twice-a-year plan (late spring and late fall) at a small discount, for example 10% off each visit when they book both. Put plan customers on your calendar first and send a reminder a week before each visit. A few dozen plan customers can fill your spring and fall weeks before you spend anything on ads.

Gutter cleaning also pairs well with other exterior services. Many customers who book gutters will also want pressure washing or window cleaning, so price a bundle.

Protect yourself with a short agreement

Before you climb, have the customer sign a short service agreement that covers:

  • What is included (gutters, downspouts, guards, haul-away)
  • That you will note but are not responsible for existing damage, loose hangers or rotted fascia
  • Photos before and after, which also make great social posts
  • Payment terms and your plan discount
