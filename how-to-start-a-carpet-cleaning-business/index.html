How to Start a Carpet Cleaning Business

Updated November 13, 2026 · 3 min read

Carpet cleaning has strong margins once your equipment is paid off, and every home with carpet, rugs or a sofa is a potential customer. It's also a natural add-on for house cleaners and handymen. Here's how to start.

1. Choose your equipment

Your biggest decision is the machine.

Option Upfront cost Good for
Portable extractor Lowest Starting out, apartments, upholstery
Truck-mounted system Highest High volume, deep extraction, faster jobs
Low-moisture or encapsulation Low to medium Commercial carpet, fast dry times

Many owners start with a quality portable extractor and an upholstery tool, then upgrade once demand is steady. Add a pre-sprayer, agitation tool (such as a counter-rotating brush), spotting kit, air movers for faster drying, hoses and corner guards.

2. Get trained

Carpet fibers and upholstery fabrics react differently to heat and chemicals. Wool, silk and some synthetics can shrink, bleed or brown. Industry certification courses teach fiber identification and safe cleaning, and customers and insurers notice them.

3. Set up the business basics

Check local business license rules, register your business name, get an EIN and open a business bank account. Buy general liability insurance, and ask about coverage for damage to customer property, since that's the main risk in this trade.

4. Set prices before your first call

Decide between per-room and per-square-foot pricing, set a minimum charge, and price stairs, upholstery and add-ons. See how much to charge for carpet cleaning for example rates.

5. Use a pre-inspection on every job

Walk the job with the customer before you start. Note permanent stains, wear, seams, color loss and furniture you won't move. Have the customer sign. This one habit prevents most disputes.

6. Leave aftercare instructions

Tell customers how long carpets take to dry, to stay off wet carpet or wear clean socks, to keep air moving, and to avoid putting furniture back on wet carpet without protectors. A printed aftercare card cuts down on callbacks.

7. Find customers

  • Google Business Profile with before-and-after photos
  • Realtors and property managers for move-out cleans
  • Pet owners through groomers, vets and pet stores
  • Seasonal promotions before the holidays and in spring
  • Reminder messages to past customers every 6 to 12 months
  • Partner with house cleaners who don't offer carpet cleaning

8. Track every job

Log jobs, payments and expenses (chemicals, fuel, equipment repairs) so you know your real profit per job. Set aside money for taxes from each payment.

Launch checklist

  • Choose and buy equipment
  • Complete fiber and cleaning training
  • License, EIN, bank account and insurance
  • Price list with minimum charge
  • Pre-inspection release, agreement and aftercare cards
  • Google Business Profile with photos
  • Job and income tracking
