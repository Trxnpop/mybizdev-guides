Mobile Detailing Prices: How to Build Your Service Menu

Updated November 13, 2026 · 3 min read

Detailing customers buy packages, not hours. A clear menu with three tiers, priced by vehicle size, makes it easy for customers to say yes and keeps you from quoting from scratch every time. Here's how to build one that's profitable.

Price by vehicle size

Bigger vehicles take longer. Most detailers use three or four size classes:

  • Small: coupes, compact cars
  • Midsize: sedans, small SUVs
  • Large: full-size SUVs, minivans, trucks
  • Extra large: third-row SUVs, lifted trucks, vans

Add a set amount or percentage for each step up in size.

Build three package tiers

Example menu structure (prices vary widely by market, so check local detailers):

Package What it includes Example price (sedan)
Maintenance wash Hand wash, wheels and tires, windows, quick interior vacuum and wipe-down $50 to $90
Full detail Exterior wash and decontamination, wax or sealant, full interior vacuum, shampoo floor mats, clean all surfaces, glass $150 to $275
Premium detail Full detail plus carpet and seat extraction, leather clean and condition, engine bay, one-step polish $250 to $450

Large vehicles often add $25 to $75 or more per tier. Mobile service usually commands a premium over a fixed shop because you come to the customer.

Charge for condition

Condition fees protect your time:

  • Pet hair removal: by time or a flat fee
  • Excessive dirt, sand or mud
  • Stain removal and odor treatment (smoke, spills)
  • Biohazard cleanup (vomit, bodily fluids), priced separately or declined

Ask about condition when booking and request photos.

High-value add-ons

  • Headlight restoration
  • Engine bay detail
  • Clay bar and paint decontamination
  • Paint correction (one-step or multi-step), priced by time
  • Ceramic coating (often several hundred dollars and up, depending on the product and prep)
  • Interior protectant and fabric guard
  • Trim restoration

Memberships for recurring income

Maintenance plans turn one-time customers into regulars: for example, a monthly or biweekly maintenance wash at a set price after an initial full detail. Membership customers are faster to service because the car never gets bad.

Inspect before you start

Walk around the vehicle with the customer and note existing scratches, dents, chips and interior damage, with photos. A vehicle inspection form prevents disputes about damage that was already there.

Water, power and logistics

Mobile detailers need to plan for water, power and runoff. Many carry water tanks and generators, use waterless or rinseless products, and check local rules about wash water runoff. Factor those costs into your prices.
