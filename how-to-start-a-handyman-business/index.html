How to Start a Handyman Business With Little Money

Updated November 12, 2026 · 3 min read

If you are good with tools and people keep asking you to fix things, you are already halfway to a handyman business. The demand is steady: homeowners, landlords and older residents all need small repairs that bigger contractors won't take. Here is how to turn that skill into a real business without a big upfront investment.

1. Learn your state's licensing limits first

This is the step people skip and regret. Many US states let you do minor repairs without a contractor's license but cap the dollar value of a single job, often somewhere between a few hundred and a few thousand dollars, including materials. Electrical, plumbing, gas and HVAC work usually require a licensed tradesperson regardless of job size. Search your state's contractor licensing board for "handyman exemption" and check your city for a general business license.

2. Set up the business basics

  • Register your business name if you are not using your own name.
  • Get an EIN from the IRS (free) so you don't hand out your Social Security number.
  • Open a business bank account.
  • Talk to an accountant about whether a sole proprietorship or LLC makes sense for you.

3. Get liability insurance

General liability insurance is a must for anyone working in other people's homes. One flooded bathroom can cost more than a year of premiums. Many property managers will not hire you without proof of insurance. If you drive a work vehicle, check whether you need commercial auto coverage too.

4. Pick your services

You don't need to do everything. Choose a core menu of jobs you do well and quickly, then expand. Popular starter services:

  • TV and shelf mounting
  • Furniture assembly
  • Drywall patching and touch-up painting
  • Caulking and grout repair
  • Faucet, toilet part and fixture replacement (within your state's rules)
  • Door and cabinet adjustments, hardware swaps
  • Gutter cleaning and minor exterior repairs
  • Smart device installs: doorbells, locks, thermostats

Write down what you won't do, too. It makes saying no easier.

5. Build a lean tool kit

Start with what you have and buy as jobs require. A solid core kit: cordless drill and impact driver, stud finder, levels, tape measure, multi-bit screwdrivers, utility knives, pliers and wrenches, a drywall kit, caulk gun, oscillating multi-tool, ladders, shop vacuum, and safety gear. Track every tool purchase as a business expense.

6. Set prices that cover your real costs

Work out your hourly rate from your costs and income goals, set a minimum charge so small trips are worth it, and decide on a materials markup. Our guide on how to price handyman jobs walks through the numbers step by step.

7. Look professional on paper

Clear paperwork wins jobs and prevents disputes. You need a job request form to capture the details, an estimate, a work order for changes, a completion sign-off, a service agreement, and an invoice. Here is how to write an estimate that wins the job.

8. Find your first customers

  • Google Business Profile. Free, and it is where people search "handyman near me." Add photos of your work.
  • Nextdoor and local Facebook groups. Post before-and-after photos and a short introduction.
  • Property managers and realtors. They need make-ready repairs and pre-listing fixes constantly.
  • Older neighbors and their families. Reliable, respectful help is in high demand.
  • Hardware store bulletin boards. Old-school, but they still work in many towns.
  • Reviews. Ask every happy client for one, the same day.

9. Keep track of the money

Log every job, payment and expense, and know which invoices are unpaid. Set aside a portion of each payment for taxes; many self-employed people put aside 25% to 30% until they know their real rate. A simple spreadsheet is enough to start.

Your launch checklist

  • Check state handyman limits and local license rules
  • Register name, get EIN, open bank account
  • Buy liability insurance
  • Choose your service menu and your "won't do" list
  • Set hourly rate, minimum and materials markup
  • Prepare estimate, work order, sign-off, agreement and invoice forms
  • Create your Google Business Profile with photos
  • Tell your network you are open
