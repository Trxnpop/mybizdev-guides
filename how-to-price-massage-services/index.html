How to Price Massage Services (Independent and Mobile Therapists)

Updated November 19, 2026 · 3 min read

Leaving a spa or chain to work for yourself usually means you keep far more of each session. It also means you're now responsible for setting prices, covering rent or travel, and filling your own calendar. Here is how independent and mobile massage therapists can build a price list that covers costs and stays competitive.

Work out your real hourly cost

Before you look at competitors, figure out what you need per hour to stay in business. Add up your monthly costs:

  • Room rent or a share of a studio (or vehicle costs if you're mobile)
  • Liability insurance and license renewals
  • Linens, laundry, oils and lotions
  • Booking software and card processing fees
  • Continuing education
  • Marketing

Then decide how many hands-on hours you can realistically work each week. Most therapists can do 15 to 25 hours of massage before their body starts to suffer, which is far fewer than a 40-hour week. Divide your costs by those hours, then add what you want to pay yourself.

Example starting rates

Massage prices vary a lot by city and setting. Check local independent therapists and spas before you settle on your own numbers. Common starting points in the US:

Service Typical starting price
60-minute massage, studio $70 to $120
90-minute massage, studio $100 to $170
30-minute focused session $45 to $70
60-minute mobile massage (at client's home) $100 to $160
Deep tissue or sports massage add $10 to $20
Prenatal massage add $0 to $15
Hot stones, cupping or aromatherapy add $10 to $25
Travel fee beyond your free radius $10 to $30

Mobile therapists charge more because they bring the table, linens and supplies, and spend time driving and setting up.

Packages and memberships

Regular clients are the key to a sustainable massage practice. Two common tools:

  • Packages: a prepaid set of 3, 5 or 10 sessions at a discount of around 10%.
  • Memberships: a monthly fee that includes one session, with extra sessions at a member rate. Members get a better price and you get predictable monthly income.

Gift certificates are another easy win, especially in November and December. Many clients buy massages as gifts, so make gift certificates easy to buy.

Policies that protect your time

Put these in writing and share them when clients book:

  • Cancellation: for example, 24 hours notice or 50% of the session price
  • No-shows: full session price
  • Late arrivals: session ends at the scheduled time
  • Payment: due at the time of service

Forms every client should complete

Every new client should fill out a massage intake form before the first session. It should cover health history, medications, injuries, areas to avoid, pressure preferences and pregnancy. Have them sign a consent form that includes your draping policy and right to end a session.

Keep SOAP notes after each session so you can track progress, especially for clients with ongoing pain or injuries. They also matter if a client ever files an insurance claim.
