How to Start an Airbnb Cleaning Business

Updated November 13, 2026 · 3 min read

Short-term rental hosts need reliable cleaners more than almost any other client. A missed turnover means a bad review, a refund and sometimes a lost listing. If you can show up on time, every time, and send proof the place is ready, hosts will keep you busy and refer you to other hosts.

1. Understand how turnovers differ from house cleaning

  • Tight windows. Usually between checkout and check-in, often a few hours.
  • Schedules change. Bookings come and go, so you work from the host's calendar.
  • Restocking and staging are part of the job.
  • Damage reporting matters. Hosts often have a limited window to file claims after checkout, and they need your photos.
  • Weekends and holidays are busy. That's when guests travel.

2. Set up the business basics

Check local business license requirements, register your business name, get an EIN and open a business bank account. Get liability insurance, and ask your agent about coverage for working in rental properties and for keys and lockbox codes. If you hire help, look into bonding.

3. Find your first hosts

  • Local host groups on Facebook and host community forums
  • Co-hosts and property managers, who manage many listings
  • Real estate agents who help investors buy rental properties
  • Search the booking sites for listings in your area and look at reviews that mention cleanliness. Those hosts may need help.
  • Your current cleaning clients may know people who host

Lead with reliability and photo reports, not price.

4. Onboard every property properly

Before your first turnover, walk through the property with the host and record:

  • Access: lockbox or door code, parking, gate codes
  • Linen setup: how many sets, where they're stored, laundry on-site or off-site
  • Supply closet: what to restock and par levels for each item
  • Staging: how beds, towels and welcome items should look (take reference photos)
  • Appliances, hot tub, trash day and recycling rules
  • Emergency contacts and the host's preferred way to communicate

A host onboarding form makes this fast and means nothing lives only in your memory.

5. Use the same checklist every time

A written turnover checklist is how you stay fast and consistent, and how you train help later. Work in the same order every visit: strip beds and start laundry first, then bathrooms, kitchen, dusting, floors, restock and stage, and take your photos last.

6. Document damage and lost items

Take photos of anything broken, stained or missing, and of items guests left behind. Send them to the host the same day with a short report. This protects the host's claim and protects you from blame.

7. Price for the real work

Price by bedrooms and bathrooms, charge for linens and same-day jobs, and offer volume discounts only for guaranteed monthly work. See how much to charge for Airbnb cleaning for example rates.

8. Get paid on a schedule

Many turnover cleaners invoice hosts weekly or twice a month for all completed turnovers. Track every turnover with the date and property so invoicing takes minutes. Put payment terms and your cancellation fee in a signed agreement.

Launch checklist

  • License, EIN, bank account and insurance
  • Turnover checklist and photo routine
  • Host onboarding form for every property
  • Restock and inventory log with par levels
  • Damage and lost item report
  • Price list with linen, same-day and add-on fees
  • Signed service agreement
  • Invoice and turnover tracking
